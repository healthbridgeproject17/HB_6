## Haemoglobin  (CBC)

**What it measures:** the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen around the body

### If low
- **Meaning:** Low haemoglobin is called anaemia: the blood carries less oxygen than usual.
- **May affect you:** Tiredness and weakness; Breathlessness on stairs or when walking; Pale skin, dizziness or headaches; A fast heartbeat
- **General self-care:** Eat iron-rich foods such as leafy greens, pulses, eggs and meat, together with vitamin C foods (lemon, orange, tomato); Avoid tea and coffee with meals, because they reduce iron absorption; Rest when you feel tired and avoid sudden heavy effort
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness; Black stools, vomiting blood, or any heavy bleeding

### If high
- **Meaning:** High haemoglobin can happen with dehydration, smoking, living at high altitude, or conditions that make the body make too many red cells.
- **May affect you:** Headache, dizziness or a flushed face
- **General self-care:** Drink enough water; If you smoke, ask for help to stop
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Chest pain, a severe headache, or weakness on one side of the body

## RBC count  (CBC)

**What it measures:** the number of red blood cells

### If low
- **Meaning:** Fewer red cells than usual, usually together with a low haemoglobin (anaemia).
- **May affect you:** Tiredness and breathlessness
- **General self-care:** Eat a balanced diet with iron-rich foods, pulses and leafy greens
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness

### If high
- **Meaning:** More red cells than usual, which can follow dehydration, smoking or low oxygen levels.
- **May affect you:** Headache or a flushed face
- **General self-care:** Drink enough water
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Chest pain or severe headache

## Haematocrit (PCV)  (CBC)

**What it measures:** the share of the blood that is made up of red cells

### If low
- **Meaning:** A low share of red cells goes with anaemia.
- **May affect you:** Tiredness and breathlessness
- **General self-care:** Eat iron-rich foods and rest when tired
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness

### If high
- **Meaning:** A high share of red cells often means dehydration, and sometimes too many red cells.
- **May affect you:** Headache or dizziness
- **General self-care:** Drink enough water
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Chest pain or severe headache

## MCV  (CBC)

**What it measures:** the average size of your red blood cells

### If low
- **Meaning:** Smaller red cells are common in iron deficiency and in some inherited blood traits.
- **May affect you:** Often only tiredness, together with anaemia
- **General self-care:** Eat iron-rich foods
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Severe tiredness or breathlessness

### If high
- **Meaning:** Larger red cells can go with low vitamin B12 or folate, alcohol use, or liver or thyroid problems.
- **May affect you:** Tiredness, tingling in hands or feet
- **General self-care:** Eat a balanced diet with eggs, dairy, pulses and green vegetables; Limit alcohol
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Numbness that spreads or trouble walking

## WBC count  (CBC)

**What it measures:** the white blood cells that fight infection

### If low
- **Meaning:** Fewer white cells than usual. Causes include viral infections, some medicines, and conditions of the bone marrow.
- **May affect you:** More frequent infections or fevers
- **General self-care:** Wash your hands often; Avoid close contact with people who are ill
- **See a doctor sooner if:** A fever of 38 C (100.4 F) or higher; A sore throat or cough that is getting worse

### If high
- **Meaning:** Often the body's response to infection or inflammation. Stress, smoking and some medicines also raise it.
- **May affect you:** Fever, tiredness or pain at the site of an infection
- **General self-care:** Rest and drink fluids; If you smoke, ask for help to stop
- **See a doctor sooner if:** A fever that does not settle; Unexplained weight loss, night sweats or bleeding

## Platelet count  (CBC)

**What it measures:** the cell fragments that help blood to clot

### If low
- **Meaning:** Platelets help blood to clot. A low count can mean easier bruising or bleeding.
- **May affect you:** Easy bruising; Nosebleeds or bleeding gums; Tiny red spots on the skin
- **General self-care:** Avoid rough sports and activities where you could be injured; Ask your doctor before using any pain-relief tablets
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Bleeding that does not stop; Black stools, blood in the urine, or a severe headache

### If high
- **Meaning:** Platelets can rise with infection, inflammation or iron deficiency.
- **May affect you:** Usually no symptoms
- **General self-care:** Drink fluids and stay active
- **See a doctor sooner if:** A sudden severe headache, chest pain, or a painful swollen leg

## ESR  (CBC)

**What it measures:** a general sign of inflammation in the body

### If high
- **Meaning:** A raised ESR points to inflammation somewhere in the body. It does not say where, and many ordinary things raise it.
- **May affect you:** Often no symptoms by itself
- **General self-care:** Rest when unwell and drink fluids
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Fever that lasts, joint swelling, or unexplained weight loss

## Fasting glucose  (Glucose)

**What it measures:** the sugar in your blood after not eating overnight

### If low
- **Meaning:** Low blood sugar can cause shakiness and sweating and can become dangerous if it falls further.
- **May affect you:** Shakiness, sweating and hunger; Confusion or fainting
- **General self-care:** If you feel these symptoms, have something sugary straight away and then a proper meal; Do not go long hours without food
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Fainting, a seizure, or not being able to swallow: Call 112 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department

### If high
- **Meaning:** Sugar is above the usual range. A fasting value of 100-125 mg/dL is the pre-diabetes range; 126 mg/dL or more on repeated tests suggests diabetes.
- **May affect you:** Feeling very thirsty and passing urine often; Tiredness; Blurred vision; Cuts that heal slowly
- **General self-care:** Cut down on sugary drinks, sweets and refined foods; Choose whole grains, pulses and vegetables, and keep portions of rice and bread moderate; Walk or do other moderate activity for about 30 minutes on most days; Do not skip meals or start a crash diet
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Very strong thirst with frequent urination, vomiting, drowsiness or confusion; Fruity-smelling breath or fast, deep breathing

## Serum creatinine  (Kidney)

**What it measures:** a waste product that healthy kidneys remove from the blood

### If high
- **Meaning:** A high creatinine suggests the kidneys are not filtering as well as they should. Dehydration and some medicines can also raise it.

- **See a doctor sooner if:** Very little urine, severe swelling, breathlessness, confusion or chest pain

**What it measures:** a waste product that can form crystals in joints when it is too high

## Serum sodium  (Electrolytes)

**What it measures:** the salt level in your blood, which controls water balance

### If low
- **Meaning:** Low sodium can follow drinking too much water, vomiting or diarrhoea, some medicines, and some illnesses.
- **May affect you:** Headache, nausea and tiredness; Muscle cramps; Confusion when it is very low
- **General self-care:** Do not drink excessive amounts of water; Do not change your salt intake sharply without advice
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Confusion, fits or fainting: Call 112 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department

### If high
- **Meaning:** High sodium usually means the body is short of water.
- **May affect you:** Strong thirst; Dry mouth and tiredness; Confusion when it is very high
- **General self-care:** Drink water regularly through the day
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Confusion, drowsiness or very little urine

## Serum potassium  (Electrolytes)

**What it measures:** a mineral that keeps the heart and muscles working

### If low
- **Meaning:** Low potassium can come from vomiting, diarrhoea, or some water tablets.
- **May affect you:** Muscle weakness and cramps; Tiredness; An irregular heartbeat when it is very low
- **General self-care:** Eat potassium-rich foods such as bananas, potatoes, pulses and coconut water, unless your doctor has told you to limit potassium

- **See a doctor sooner if:** Palpitations, chest pain, severe weakness or fainting: Call 112 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department

- **General self-care:** Get some morning sunlight on your arms and legs, a few times a week; Eat eggs, fish and fortified milk; Ask your doctor whether you need a supplement and for how long
- **See a doctor sooner if:** A bone that hurts after a small fall; Severe muscle weakness

## Vitamin B12  (Vitamins)

**What it measures:** vitamin B12, which the body needs for nerves and red blood cells

### If low
- **Meaning:** Low vitamin B12 can cause anaemia and affect the nerves.
- **May affect you:** Tiredness; Tingling or numbness in the hands and feet; Memory or mood changes; A sore tongue
- **General self-care:** Eat eggs, dairy, fish or meat; people who eat no animal foods should ask their doctor about fortified foods or supplements
- **See a doctor sooner if:** Numbness that is spreading, or trouble walking

## Combinations

- **iron_deficiency:** Low haemoglobin with small red cells and/or low iron stores is the typical picture of iron deficiency anaemia.  Questions: What is causing my low iron (food, periods or bleeding)? / Do I need treatment, and when should the blood test be repeated?
- **kidney_impairment:** Creatinine, urea and/or eGFR together suggest the kidneys are not filtering as well as they should.  Questions: How well are my kidneys working and what is the cause? / Do I need a urine test, an ultrasound, or a kidney specialist?
- **hyperkalaemia_kidney:** When the kidneys are weak, potassium can build up and disturb the heart rhythm. This combination needs same-day medical attention.  Questions: Do I need a heart tracing (ECG) and a repeat potassium test today?
- **low_counts:** Several types of blood cell are low together. A doctor will want to find the reason.  Questions: Why are several of my blood counts low, and do I need more tests?
- **diabetes_range:** The blood sugar results are in the diabetes range.  Questions: Do I have diabetes, and do the tests need repeating? / What should I change in my food and activity, and when should I be checked again?
- **prediabetes:** Blood sugar is above normal but below the diabetes range. Lifestyle changes can often bring it back down.  Questions: What can I do to lower my sugar, and when should I be tested again?
- **hypothyroid:** A raised TSH, especially with a low free T4, points to an underactive thyroid.  Questions: Do I need treatment for my thyroid, and when should the test be repeated?
- **hyperthyroid:** A low TSH with a high free T4 points to an overactive thyroid.  Questions: What is causing my overactive thyroid, and do I need more tests?
- **mixed_lipids:** Both LDL cholesterol and triglycerides are high, which raises the long-term risk to the heart.  Questions: What is my heart risk, and do I need treatment besides diet and exercise?
- **liver_enzymes:** The liver enzymes are well above normal, which can point to liver irritation or injury.  Questions: What is irritating my liver, and do I need a scan or more tests?
